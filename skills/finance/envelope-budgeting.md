# Envelope Budgeting & Personal Finance Fundamentals

Envelope budgeting assigns every income dollar to a named purpose before the month begins. When the envelope is empty, spending in that category stops. It makes trade-offs visible and converts vague intentions into concrete constraints.

## The zero-based budget

Every dollar in = every dollar assigned (to spending, saving, or debt). Monthly income minus all assigned amounts = 0. This is not "spend everything" — savings envelopes are mandatory assignments.

```
Monthly take-home income
  − Fixed expenses (rent, loan payments, subscriptions)
  − Variable necessities (groceries, utilities, transport)
  − Savings goals (emergency fund, specific targets)
  − Sinking funds (see below)
  − Wants/discretionary
  ─────────────────────
  = 0 (every dollar named)
```

If the result is negative: reduce discretionary first, then revisit variable necessities, then question fixed costs. If positive: assign the surplus to a savings goal.

## The 50/30/20 rule

A simpler starting framework for users who want structure without per-category tracking:

| Category | Target | What's included |
|----------|--------|----------------|
| **Needs** (50%) | Rent, utilities, groceries, transport, minimum debt payments | Non-negotiable survival costs |
| **Wants** (30%) | Dining out, entertainment, subscriptions, shopping | Everything you could live without |
| **Savings/debt** (20%) | Emergency fund, investments, extra debt repayment | Pay yourself first |

Use 50/30/20 as a diagnostic: if needs exceed 50%, the underlying issue is income vs. fixed cost, not self-control. Addressing the former matters more than tightening the latter.

## Sinking funds: planned irregular expenses

Irregular expenses (car maintenance, annual subscriptions, holiday gifts, medical copays) wreck budgets because they feel unexpected but aren't. A sinking fund divides a future cost into monthly contributions:

```
Monthly contribution = total cost / months until needed
```

Example: €600 holiday budget in 8 months → €75/month sinking fund.

Common sinking fund categories: car, home maintenance, medical, travel, gifts, clothing, electronics.

## Emergency fund

**Purpose:** absorb genuine emergencies (job loss, medical, major repair) without debt. **Target:** 3–6 months of essential expenses, in a liquid account, never invested. **Priority:** build a €1,000 starter fund before any aggressive debt repayment; then return to it after debt is cleared.

The emergency fund is a system component, not a goal in itself — it prevents the budget from collapsing under unexpected shock.

## Debt sequencing

Two defensible strategies:

| Strategy | Method | Best for |
|----------|--------|----------|
| **Avalanche** | Pay highest interest rate first | Mathematically optimal; saves most money |
| **Snowball** | Pay smallest balance first | Psychologically effective; builds momentum |

For users struggling with motivation, snowball often works better despite being suboptimal on paper. Pick the one the user will actually stick to.

## Behavioral principles

- **Name every category.** Generic "miscellaneous" envelopes become black holes. Force specificity.
- **Budget before the month starts.** Reactive budgeting is 80% of why budgets fail.
- **Don't shame past overspending.** The budget resets each month. Analysis without judgment.
- **Track every transaction in real time**, not in retrospect. Delayed tracking leads to estimates and missed items.
- **Buffer month vs. current month:** ideally, this month's spending comes from last month's income (true zero-based). For beginners, start with current-month income and move to the buffer model once a one-month cushion exists.

## Scope boundary

A budget companion tracks, plans, and coaches. It does not: give investment advice (asset allocation, security selection), advise on tax strategy, recommend specific financial products (banks, funds, insurance), or give legal/debt-relief guidance. For complex situations (bankruptcy, divorce, significant assets), recommend a licensed financial planner or advisor.
