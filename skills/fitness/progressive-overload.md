# Progressive Overload & Training Principles

Progressive overload is the foundational principle of physical adaptation: the body adapts to a training stimulus only if that stimulus is incrementally increased over time. Without it, improvement plateaus. With too much, injury or overtraining results.

## FITT Framework

Every training prescription has four dimensions to manipulate:

| Variable | Definition | How to progress |
|----------|-----------|----------------|
| **Frequency** | Sessions per week | Add a day after 4–6 weeks at the same frequency |
| **Intensity** | Load (% of 1RM) or RPE | Increase weight when top-end reps feel easy |
| **Time** | Session duration / volume (sets × reps) | Add a set, or add 5 min cardio |
| **Type** | Exercise selection / modality | Change exercises every 4–8 weeks for continued stimulus |

**The rule of 10%:** increase any single FITT variable by no more than 10% per week to avoid overuse injury.

## 1RM Estimation (Epley Formula)

When a user hasn't tested their one-rep max, estimate it from a submaximal effort:

```
1RM = weight × (1 + reps / 30)
```

Example: lifts 60 kg for 8 reps → estimated 1RM = 60 × (1 + 8/30) = 76 kg

**Use for programming, not competition.** Accuracy degrades above ~12 reps.

## Rep Range and Intensity Zones

| Goal | % of 1RM | Rep range | Rest between sets |
|------|----------|-----------|-------------------|
| Max strength | 85–100% | 1–5 | 3–5 min |
| Hypertrophy | 65–85% | 6–12 | 1–2 min |
| Muscular endurance | 50–65% | 12–20+ | <1 min |
| Power | 30–60% (explosive) | 1–6 | 3–5 min |

Most beginners build strength and hypertrophy simultaneously across all rep ranges — don't over-engineer zones for novices.

## RPE Scale (Rate of Perceived Exertion)

When % of 1RM is impractical (bodyweight training, variable equipment), use RPE 1–10:

| RPE | Meaning |
|-----|---------|
| 10 | Maximum effort, could not do another rep |
| 9 | Could do 1 more rep |
| 8 | Could do 2–3 more (leaving reps in reserve) |
| 7 | Could do 4–5 more |
| ≤6 | Warm-up territory |

Working sets typically target RPE 7–9. RPE 10 every session is a path to overtraining.

## Periodization

**Linear (beginner):** add weight every session. Works until ~3 months.

**Wave loading (intermediate):** rotate intensity week to week. E.g., Week 1: 75% / Week 2: 80% / Week 3: 85% / Week 4: deload at 60%.

**Deload:** a planned week of reduced volume (~40–50%) every 4–6 weeks. Essential — not optional. Deloads are when adaptation is consolidated.

## Recovery and DOMS

DOMS (Delayed Onset Muscle Soreness) peaks 24–48h after novel exercise. It is NOT a reliable indicator of training quality — absent DOMS doesn't mean a poor session; severe DOMS may indicate too much too soon.

Recovery guidelines:
- Same muscle group: 48h minimum between hard sessions
- Sleep: the single most important recovery factor (7–9h)
- Nutrition: protein synthesis peaks at ~0.4g/kg per meal, ~1.6–2.2g/kg/day total
- Soreness that persists beyond 5 days or is joint pain (not muscle) = rest and reassess

## Scope boundary: medical conditions

Progressive overload principles apply to healthy adults. Do not prescribe training intensity for users who mention: recent surgery, injury, pregnancy, diagnosed cardiovascular disease, osteoporosis, or any condition where exercise has been restricted by a physician. Refer to a qualified trainer or physical therapist. Scope out immediately — do not attempt to work around medical contraindications.
