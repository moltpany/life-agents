# Communicative Language Learning & Corrective Feedback

Communicative Language Teaching (CLT) positions authentic communication — not grammar drills — as both the means and goal of language learning. Fluency grows through meaningful interaction; accuracy grows through noticing and feedback.

## Krashen's i+1: comprehensible input

Language is acquired when input is one step above the learner's current level — neither too easy (no new exposure) nor too far beyond (incomprehensible). The "i+1" principle guides conversation difficulty:

- Speak at the learner's level with occasional new vocabulary in clear context
- Rephrase if comprehension breaks down; don't repeat louder
- Grammar explanations are secondary to meaningful exchange — most learners acquire structure implicitly through use

## Fluency vs. accuracy: choose one per moment

Correcting every error during a fluency activity destroys the communicative purpose. The agent must choose its stance before each activity:

| Mode | Goal | Correction approach |
|------|------|-------------------|
| **Fluency mode** | Communicating ideas | Let errors pass; note patterns for a focused post-activity review |
| **Accuracy mode** | Form practice | Provide immediate corrective feedback; slow down |
| **Hybrid (conversation)** | Natural exchange | Recast (see below) without interrupting; explicit correction for fossilizable errors only |

## Corrective feedback techniques

| Technique | What it looks like | When to use |
|-----------|-------------------|-------------|
| **Recast** | Learner: "Yesterday I go to market." Agent: "Oh, you went to the market! What did you buy?" | Default in conversation — corrects without interrupting flow |
| **Explicit correction** | "Actually, we say 'went' — 'go' is the present form." | Persistent error after multiple recasts; fossilizable patterns |
| **Elicitation** | "Yesterday, you…?" (with rising intonation, inviting self-correction) | When the learner almost certainly knows the rule |
| **Clarification request** | "Sorry, I didn't quite follow — could you say that again?" | When the meaning, not just the form, is unclear |
| **Metalinguistic hint** | "Is that the right tense here?" | When the learner has studied the rule but isn't applying it |

**Recasting is the workhorse** for conversation-mode practice. Explicit correction belongs in accuracy-focused drills, or for recurring errors that won't self-correct.

## Productive error vs. fossilization

Errors are evidence of hypothesis-testing — a sign the learner is active. Most errors self-correct with sufficient input. **Fossilization** is the exception: an error pattern that persists unchanged despite exposure and feedback, often because it doesn't impede communication. Common examples: third-person -s omission ("she go"), copula omission in informal speech. Fossilizable errors warrant more explicit focus.

## Vocabulary acquisition: comprehensible encounters

A word is acquired after ~10–15 comprehensible encounters in meaningful context (Nation's word frequency research). Implications:
- Recycle new vocabulary across the conversation, not in isolation
- Glossing (brief inline definition) beats interrupting the flow for a dictionary check
- Deliberate vocabulary review (flashcards, spaced repetition) supplements; it doesn't replace contextual exposure

## Pronunciation feedback

Correct pronunciation errors when they:
- Change meaning (e.g., ship/sheep, live/leave in English)
- Are likely to confuse native speakers

Don't correct accent unless the learner explicitly asks. Accent reduction is a specific, separate goal. In conversation mode, model the correct pronunciation naturally in your next turn rather than stopping to drill.

## Session structure

For a 30–60 min practice session:
1. **Warm-up** (5 min): free conversation on a comfortable topic — lowers affective filter
2. **Focused activity** (20–30 min): chosen topic or scenario; agent adjusts difficulty in real time
3. **Language focus** (10–15 min): review of patterns noted during the session; accuracy exercises if needed
4. **Wrap-up** (5 min): what was hard, what was new, what to practice before next session
