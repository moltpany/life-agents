# Houseplant Symptom Diagnosis

Diagnosis is differential: the same symptom (yellow leaves) has opposite causes (over- vs. underwatering) with opposite fixes. Never prescribe from a single symptom — locate it, then confirm with a second signal.

## Diagnostic protocol

1. **Locate the symptom precisely.** Old lower leaves vs. new growth vs. all-over; leaf tips vs. edges vs. center; one side vs. whole plant.
2. **Rank 2–3 likely causes** by probability given the plant type and care history.
3. **Name the confirming check** that separates the causes (soil moisture at depth, root inspection, undersides of leaves).
4. **Give the fix per cause** — and what to expect if the fix is working (timeline matters; recovery shows in new growth, damaged leaves don't heal).

## Differential table

| Symptom | Most likely | Second guess | Confirming check |
|---------|------------|--------------|------------------|
| Lower leaves yellowing slowly, one at a time | Normal aging | Mild overwatering | Rate: 1 leaf/month is aging; several at once + wet soil is water |
| Many leaves yellowing at once, soil stays wet | Overwatering / early root rot | Poor drainage | Soil smell (sour = rot), roots brown & mushy vs. white & firm |
| Yellowing on new growth | Nutrient deficiency (iron/manganese if veins stay green) | Root damage | Fertilizing history; root check |
| Brown crispy leaf tips | Low humidity | Fertilizer salt buildup / tap water fluoride (dracaena, spider plant) | Humidity level; white crust on soil surface |
| Brown soft patches | Overwatering / fungal | Cold damage | Soil moisture; recent cold drafts |
| Drooping despite wet soil | Root rot (roots can't drink) | Compacted airless soil | Unpot and inspect roots — this one is urgent |
| Drooping with dry soil | Underwatering | Rootbound | Recovery within hours of watering confirms; roots circling the pot = rootbound |
| Leggy growth, small pale new leaves | Insufficient light | — | Distance/direction of nearest window |
| Sudden leaf drop | Shock (move, draft, repot) | Watering extreme | Recent changes in the last 2 weeks |
| Fine webbing, stippled leaves | Spider mites | — | White paper test: tap leaf, look for moving dots |
| Small flies from soil | Fungus gnats (overwatered top layer) | — | Yellow sticky trap; let top 3 cm dry fully |
| Sticky residue, brown bumps on stems | Scale insects | Aphids (on new growth) | Bumps scrape off with fingernail = scale |
| White cottony clusters in leaf joints | Mealybugs | — | Visual; dab with isopropyl alcohol swab |

## Treatment notes

- **Root rot:** unpot, cut all brown/mushy roots with clean scissors, repot in fresh dry mix, water lightly, no fertilizer until new growth. Survival is not guaranteed — say so honestly.
- **Pests (mites, scale, mealybugs):** isolate the plant first. Treat with insecticidal soap or neem oil, repeat every 5–7 days for 3 cycles to catch hatchlings. One treatment never suffices.
- **Fungus gnats:** bottom-water only, let the top layer dry, sticky traps for adults; BTI (mosquito bits) for persistent cases.
- **Damaged leaves never recover.** Trim them for the plant's energy budget and the owner's morale, and judge recovery by new growth only.

## Escalation boundary

Symptoms that may require lab testing or are beyond home diagnosis: suspected bacterial/viral infection (mosaic patterns, water-soaked lesions spreading fast), anything affecting commercially valuable collections, edible crops with suspected soil contamination. Refer out rather than guess.
