# Houseplant Watering Fundamentals

Overwatering kills more houseplants than underwatering. Correct watering is a function of plant type, pot, soil, light, and season — never a fixed calendar alone. A schedule is a *prompt to check*, not a command to pour.

## Baseline intervals by plant type

Starting points for a 6" pot in moderate indirect light, average home humidity, growing season:

| Plant group | Interval | Signal to water |
|-------------|----------|----------------|
| Succulents & cacti | 14–21 days | Soil fully dry throughout pot |
| Tropical foliage (pothos, monstera, philodendron) | 7–10 days | Top 2–3 cm dry |
| Ferns & moisture-lovers (calathea, peace lily) | 4–6 days | Surface just drying, never bone-dry |
| Mediterranean herbs (rosemary, thyme) | 5–7 days | Top 2–3 cm dry; drainage is critical |
| Snake plants & ZZ plants | 14–28 days | Soil fully dry; err on the long side |

## Adjustment factors

Apply to the baseline, multiplicatively in spirit (they compound):

| Factor | Adjustment |
|--------|-----------|
| Pot size 4" or smaller | Shorten interval ~30–40% (small pots dry fast) |
| Pot size 10" or larger | Lengthen ~30–50% |
| Terracotta pot | Shorten ~20% (breathes, wicks moisture) |
| Plastic/glazed pot | Lengthen ~20% |
| No drainage hole | Do not follow a schedule at all — water sparingly by feel, recommend repotting |
| Bright direct light / south window | Shorten ~20–30% |
| Low light / north window | Lengthen ~30–50% |
| Winter (non-growing season) | Lengthen ~50% and reduce volume; growth dormancy = low uptake |
| Heating season / humidity < 40% | Shorten ~20% for thin-leaved tropicals |
| Chunky/airy soil mix | Shorten slightly |
| Dense/water-retentive mix | Lengthen slightly |

**Always ask about pot size, pot material, drainage, and light before giving watering advice.** A monstera in a 4" terracotta pot in a south window and the same plant in a 12" plastic pot in a dim corner differ by a factor of 3–4×.

## The check that beats every schedule

Finger test: insert a finger 2–3 cm into the soil. Dry at depth → water. Moist → wait. For large pots, a wooden chopstick (comes out clean = dry) or lifting the pot to judge weight is more reliable.

## How to water

- Water thoroughly until it runs from the drainage hole, then empty the saucer after 15–30 minutes. Standing water = root rot.
- Shallow frequent sips train shallow roots and leave deep soil dry; deep infrequent watering is almost always correct.
- Room-temperature water; very cold water shocks tropical roots.

## Seasonal rhythm

- **Spring:** uptake increases; return to growing-season intervals, resume fertilizing.
- **Summer:** peak demand; check more often during heat waves.
- **Autumn:** taper intervals and stop fertilizing as light drops.
- **Winter:** most houseplants are semi-dormant. The most common winter mistake is keeping the summer schedule — this is when root rot happens.
